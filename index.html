02 / Spudnik Equipment · Summer 2024

Mechanical design & reliability

A simpler mount.
A more reliable drivetrain.

A sugar beet harvester’s chain drive was difficult to tension, assemble, and service. I redesigned the layout and mounting arrangement to address those problems together.

10 fewer partsin the redesigned assembly

NX CAD · Design for assembly · Chain tensioning · Hardware validation

See it running ↘
Behind the design Problem, decisions, and validation+

The problem

The drive suffered from chain skipping and whip. A low point in the chain path also created guard and mud interference. Stacked steel plates and through-bolts made assembly awkward in a confined space.

My contribution

I investigated the failure modes and iterated layouts in NX. I raised the chain’s low point, incorporated tensioning arms, and simplified the mounting arrangement to improve assembly access.

Validation & result

After implementation, the system ran at speed without the chain skipping observed previously. The assembly used ten fewer parts. This result describes the design from my internship, rather than the machine’s current production configuration.

What I took forward

Reliability and service access belong in the same design discussion. Working with the hardware helped me see constraints that were less obvious on a CAD screen.

Behind the integration My role and the production constraints+

The constraint

The station needed automatic grading without adding an awkward manual step. Glare, reflective surfaces, and movement in front of the label affected the initial setup.

My contribution

I tested printing and scanning at an angle, designed the angled printer stand, and helped install the updated hardware. I worked alongside the robotics lead on vision setup and the automatic grading sequence.

The outcome

Labels could be scanned automatically as they printed, removing the separate handheld scan and reducing the workflow by approximately ten seconds. The installation supported the required grading process.

Why it matters to me

This is the kind of engineering I enjoy: a mechanical change that makes a sensor and a production process work better together.

Beyond the printer

Make the inspection
work on the line.

I designed scanner mounts and helped install updated scanning hardware for the battery label changes. I also updated label jigs, checked label content, prepared temporary work instructions, and trained team members on the revised process.

The work extended into error-log comparison tools, HV-cap cleaning and preventive maintenance, label-roll guides, and production layout updates for reduced-speed operation.
